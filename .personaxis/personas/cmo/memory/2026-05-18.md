## episodic

- Day 3 of the positioning sprint. Five buyer interviews complete. Pattern read from week 1 is confirmed: this is a positioning problem.
- Three of five buyers (won deals) named "the spreadsheet plus a junior on compliance" as the alternative they rejected. Two named "Big Four consultant." Zero named ComplyAdvantage as a real alternative. Confirms the real competitive set.
- The strongest customer quote came from VP Compliance at a Series B neobank: "My regulator does not care that I monitored the transactions. They care that I can prove I was monitoring when something happened." This becomes the keynote anchor for the Q3 launch and the worldview the company invests in owning.
- ICP sharpened from "fintech startups in PSD2 / AML" to "Heads of Compliance at PSD2 / AML-regulated fintechs (20-150 employees), preparing for their first formal regulator engagement." Trigger event is the unlock. CEO confirmed the sharpening.
- Locked positioning statement drafted (in the canonical Dunford structure). Three banned phrases identified: "AI-powered", "streamline your compliance", "reduce risk" as primary claim. Three required phrases identified: "continuous compliance", "provable continuous monitoring", "15 minutes a month".
- Decided category strategy: subdivide RegTech under "continuous compliance" rather than create a new category. 6-12 month vocabulary investment, not a 3-5 year category arc. CEO agreed; capital and patience do not support category creation right now.

## user_preferences

- User confirmed the writing should be his (CEO) voice on external surfaces. CMO drafts; CEO edits the public-facing pieces. Sales enablement is CMO-owned end to end.
- User dislikes hedging. When the CMO says "this is high confidence," he expects no equivocation in the recommendation. When confidence is genuinely lower, name the number, not the hedge.
- User does not want to be told that something "needs to be tested" without a specific test design. Test design includes thesis, lead measure, kill criterion.
