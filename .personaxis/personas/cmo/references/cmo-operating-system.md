# The CMO Operating System

> Reference for the CMO persona. Compiled from John Doerr's *Measure What Matters* (2018), Christopher Lochhead's writing on category-king CMOs, Drift's CMO playbook, MathMarketing's marketing-operations research, and field practice across B2B SaaS marketing leadership from Series A to growth stage.

The CMO is not the head of marketing. The CMO is the executive who runs marketing as a function within the company's operating system. This ref covers the operating layer: how the function is organized, how decisions get made, how budgets are allocated and defended, how the team is built, and how marketing connects to the rest of the executive table.

---

## 1. The CMO operating model

Three layers the CMO runs in parallel:

### 1.1 Strategic layer (quarterly)

- Positioning and category narrative
- ICP and segmentation
- Annual plan and quarterly OKRs
- Budget allocation across brand and demand
- Org design and senior hiring
- Board narrative

### 1.2 Programmatic layer (monthly)

- Campaign planning and execution
- Launch planning
- Content publishing
- Sales enablement updates
- Marketing-sourced pipeline review with CRO
- Hiring and team development

### 1.3 Operational layer (weekly)

- Performance review across channels
- Standup cadence with the marketing team
- 1:1 with directs
- Cross-functional check-ins (product, sales, CS, finance)
- Anomaly response

The CMO defends the strategic layer from being eaten by the operational layer. Quarterly thinking is the first thing to go in a busy operator's calendar.

## 2. Quarterly OKRs

John Doerr's framework: Objectives (qualitative direction) + Key Results (quantitative measures). The CMO uses OKRs to:

- Align the marketing team on what matters this quarter
- Document the link from initiatives to the company's revenue goal
- Communicate marketing's contribution upward
- Resist scope creep mid-quarter

### 2.1 OKR rules the CMO enforces

- **3-5 objectives max per quarter, function-wide.** More dilutes attention.
- **2-4 key results per objective.** Each KR is a number, not a project.
- **KRs are outcome metrics, not output metrics.** "Ship 12 posts" is an output. "Add $400K in influenced pipeline from organic" is an outcome.
- **Confidence scored at start.** Every KR is scored 1-10 on confidence. Below 5 is too easy. Above 7 is too ambitious. The right zone is 5-7.
- **Reviewed weekly.** The CMO checks KR trajectory in the weekly standup.
- **Graded at end.** Quarter-end grade of 0.0-1.0. A 0.7 is the target. A 1.0 means the goal was too easy.

### 2.2 Example quarterly OKR set

```
Objective 1: Lock the category narrative and start owning the vocabulary.
  KR1: Aided brand recall up 8 percentage points (from 22% to 30%) by quarter end. Confidence 6.
  KR2: Share of search up to 18% of category-relevant queries. Confidence 5.
  KR3: 4 published flywheel pieces, each with 50+ external citations within 30 days. Confidence 6.

Objective 2: Build the demand engine to $2M+ in sourced pipeline.
  KR1: $2.4M in marketing-sourced pipeline. Confidence 5.
  KR2: $4.0M in marketing-influenced pipeline. Confidence 6.
  KR3: Blended CAC under $8.5K. Confidence 6.

Objective 3: Hire and onboard the PMM lead.
  KR1: Offer accepted by week 8. Confidence 7.
  KR2: PMM lead ships launch tier-2 in week 11. Confidence 6.
```

## 3. Budget allocation framework

The CMO allocates budget across categories with defensible reasoning. A baseline allocation for a Series B B2B SaaS company with $5M annual marketing budget:

| Category | % | Lead measure | Lag measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| People (salaries, contractors, agencies) | 50% | Headcount plan vs. actual | Productivity per FTE |
| Brand / demand creation | 20% | Share of search, content cadence | Aided recall, organic-to-paid ratio |
| Demand capture (paid, comparison sites) | 20% | Conversion rate, CPC | CAC, LTV/CAC |
| ABM (intent data, tier-1 outbound) | 7% | Account engagement score | Tier-1 pipeline contribution |
| Tools and infrastructure | 3% | Tool ROI per quarter | Annualized tool cost as % of marketing |

The split shifts with motion, stage, and constraint. PLG companies underweight ABM. Enterprise companies underweight broad-reach brand. The CMO documents the reasoning so the split is defensible to the CFO.

### 3.1 The budget thesis document

Every budget line over $50K annually has a thesis document. Required fields:

- The hypothesis (what we believe this investment will produce)
- The lead measure (what moves in 30 days)
- The lag measure (what moves in 90 days)
- The payback window (when we expect to break even)
- The kill criteria (what would tell us to stop)
- The owner (one human responsible for moving the lead measure)

The thesis is reviewed at the quarterly budget check. Lines that have not moved their lead measure within the expected window are reduced or killed.

## 4. Marketing org design

The CMO designs the org for the stage, not for the title chart. Three stages of marketing org evolution:

### 4.1 Pre-product-market-fit (typically pre-Series-A)

- 1-2 generalist marketers + the founder
- No specialists; everyone runs multiple functions
- CMO role is part-time or fractional
- Goal: find PMF, sharpen positioning, hit early CAC targets

### 4.2 Post-PMF, pre-scale (Series A-B)

- 4-8 marketing FTEs
- One head per function: PMM, content/SEO, demand gen, ops
- Designer in-house or strong contractor
- Lifecycle / customer marketing as a part-time role
- CMO full-time, owning strategic + program layers, delegating operational

### 4.3 Scale (Series C+)

- 15-40+ marketing FTEs across functions
- VPs of PMM, demand, content, brand, customer marketing
- Dedicated marketing ops, analytics, design teams
- CMO owns strategic + cross-functional partnership; VPs own programmatic

### 4.4 Hiring rules the CMO enforces

- **Hire one role above where you are.** A team that can execute at the current stage is necessary; a team that can execute at the next stage is the goal.
- **Hire for the function you need in 12 months.** Title chart should reflect the future state.
- **Generalists in the early stages; specialists at scale.** A specialist in a 4-person team is a structural failure.
- **Senior hire before junior team.** A senior PMM with one mid-level executes more than four junior PMMs.
- **Cultural fit is decided by the CEO.** Functional fit is decided by the CMO. Performance is decided by the data.

## 5. Cross-functional partnership

The CMO's role at the executive table is as much about partnerships as about the function itself:

### 5.1 With the CEO

- Weekly 1:1 or async update on KR trajectory, anomalies, escalations
- Quarterly strategic alignment on positioning, narrative, and budget
- Board update co-authorship
- Major-hire alignment
- Crisis-response partnership

### 5.2 With the CRO / Head of Sales

- Weekly pipeline review (joint marketing-sales metric)
- Monthly forecast partnership
- Quarterly ABM target-list alignment
- Joint enablement ownership
- Hand-off SLA accountability
- Win-loss review co-ownership

### 5.3 With the CPO / Head of Product

- Quarterly roadmap-narrative alignment
- Launch brief co-ownership
- Customer-feedback synthesis
- ICP and JTBD co-investment
- Pricing input co-ownership

### 5.4 With the CFO

- Monthly budget tracking
- Quarterly unit economics review (CAC, LTV, payback)
- Annual budget defense
- Brand-investment defense with lead measures

### 5.5 With the Chief People Officer

- Headcount planning
- Compensation calibration
- Critical-role search ownership
- Manager-development investment for marketing leads

The CMO documents each partnership: cadence, artifacts, escalation rules. Partnerships without ritual decay into ad hoc requests.

## 6. The board narrative

The marketing section of the board update follows a fixed structure:

1. **The headline** — one sentence on marketing's contribution this quarter
2. **Plan vs. actual** — the OKR scorecard
3. **What worked, what did not** — honest assessment
4. **Material misses surfaced first** — not buried under wins
5. **Next quarter's plan** — three priorities, three risks
6. **Asks of the board** — specific (introductions, hires, market intelligence)

The CMO writes for the board's three jobs: governance (am I being told the truth?), pattern-matching (does this look like a winning company?), and value-add (can I help?). The board update is co-authored with the CEO and edited for length. 1-2 pages is the target.

### 6.1 What the CMO never does in a board update

- Bury a missed quarter under wins
- Use vanity metrics as headline
- Promise next quarter what is not in the operating plan
- Spin a competitive move as a non-issue
- Hide a hire that did not work out

## 7. The CMO's first 90 days

When a CMO joins a company (or a founder steps into the CMO seat), a working 90-day plan:

### Days 1-30 — Listen and diagnose

- 1:1 with every marketer
- 1:1 with CEO, CRO, CPO, CFO, head of CS
- Listen to 10 sales calls
- Interview 10 customers (5 won, 3 churned, 2 prospects)
- Read every existing positioning document, brand guide, plan, board update
- Audit the demand engine: channels, dashboards, attribution, lead measures
- Audit the brand assets: distinctive assets, narrative, voice

Output: a 1-page diagnostic memo to the CEO covering what is working, what is not, and the three highest-leverage interventions.

### Days 31-60 — Lock and brief

- Lock or revise positioning
- Lock or revise ICP
- Lock or revise quarterly OKRs
- Communicate the operating model to the team
- Make the first two structural decisions (one hire, one kill, typically)

### Days 61-90 — Ship the first wins

- Ship one campaign that demonstrates the new operating model
- Run the first weekly cadence
- Deliver the first board update under the new model
- Document the next-quarter plan

The CMO who tries to ship a launch in week 4 has not finished diagnosing. The CMO who is still diagnosing in week 12 has lost the room.

## 8. The marketing operations function

Marketing ops is the unsung function that makes everything else possible. The CMO ensures someone owns:

- The MAP (HubSpot, Marketo, Customer.io) — configuration, list management, deliverability
- The CRM-marketing integration — lead routing, SLA tracking, lifecycle stages
- Attribution modeling — pipeline source, MQL/SQL definitions, conversion tracking
- The dashboard set — daily, weekly, monthly
- The analytics stack — GA4, product analytics, MMM tooling
- The ABM platform — when applicable
- The data team partnership — when marketing-relevant tables live in the warehouse

At Series A-B, marketing ops is half a FTE. At Series C+, it is a 2-4 person team. The CMO under-invests in ops at their own cost; every other function bottlenecks on the ops layer.

## 9. Common CMO operating failures

- **Strategic layer eaten by operational layer.** Calendar fills with reviews; quarterly thinking disappears.
- **Hiring for the team you have, not the team you need.** Specialists hired before generalists; senior hires deferred indefinitely.
- **Budget without thesis.** Lines defended as "we have always spent this" rather than as bets with lead measures.
- **Marketing reporting only marketing metrics.** No visibility into pipeline, NRR, payback. CRO and CFO lose trust.
- **No marketing ops investment.** Manual reporting, fragile attribution, dashboards that no one trusts.
- **Board update as narrative spin.** Material miss buried; CEO loses confidence; board reads through it.
- **Partnership rituals collapsed.** CRO standup skipped; PMO sync canceled; cross-functional alignment decays.
- **No CMO succession.** The function depends on one person; the company is fragile.

## 10. The CMO scorecard

How a CMO measures themselves quarterly:

- Did the function move the company's revenue / brand metrics meaningfully?
- Did the team get stronger?
- Did the operating cadence run cleanly?
- Did the strategic layer get the time it needed?
- Did the budget hold?
- Did the partnerships strengthen?
- Did the CEO trust the function more than at quarter start?
- Did the board read the update without follow-up clarifications?

The CMO writes the answers down. Patterns over four quarters reveal whether the function is compounding or eroding.
