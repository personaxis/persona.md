# Demand Generation Playbook

> Reference for the CMO persona. Compiled from Sangram Vajre's *ABM is B2B* (2019), Chris Walker's writing on demand creation vs. demand capture, the 95-5 rule (Ehrenberg-Bass), and field practice across PLG, SLG, and hybrid motions.

Demand generation is the function that turns positioning, brand, and product into measurable pipeline. The CMO distinguishes **demand creation** (making buyers care about the problem) from **demand capture** (closing buyers who already care). Most B2B marketing over-invests in capture and under-invests in creation, then complains about CAC.

---

## 1. The 95-5 rule

At any given moment, ~95% of B2B category buyers are not in-market. They are not searching. They are not comparing. They will be in-market on a future trigger — a new hire, a contract renewal, a missed audit, a strategic priority shift.

This produces two distinct marketing jobs:

| Job | Audience | Mechanism | Time horizon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand creation** | The 95% (out-of-market) | Reach, narrative, mental availability, brand | 6-24 months |
| **Demand capture** | The 5% (in-market) | Search, comparison sites, retargeting, sales outreach | 0-3 months |

A demand engine that only does capture wins the share of a fixed in-market pool and runs out of pool. A demand engine that does both grows the in-market pool over time. The CMO budgets both.

## 2. The Chris Walker reframe — dark social and intent

Chris Walker's argument: B2B buyers do their research in places marketing cannot measure (Slack DMs, peer conversations, podcasts, LinkedIn feed, executive newsletters). They arrive at the website "ready to buy," and the form fill is credited to the last-click channel.

Implications for the CMO:

1. **Last-click attribution dramatically over-credits paid search and direct.** A buyer who heard the founder on a podcast for 18 months and finally Googled the brand is credited to "branded search."
2. **The right metric is not MQL volume; it is sales-influenced pipeline by source.** Ask the AE on every deal: *"How did they first hear about us?"*
3. **Self-reported attribution beats platform attribution.** A 4-question form ("How did you hear about us?" with open-text) outperforms multi-touch attribution for strategic decisions.
4. **Demand creation lives in places that do not produce attributable conversions.** Podcasts, LinkedIn thought leadership, in-person events, executive newsletters, peer communities.

The CMO commits to investing in demand creation despite imperfect attribution. Refusing to invest in unattributable channels is a recipe for losing to a competitor who invests in them.

## 3. The demand engine — five layers

The CMO builds the demand engine in layers, each with distinct mechanics:

### Layer 1 — Foundation (always-on)
- Website and pricing page that ship the positioning
- Owned content (blog, podcast, newsletter) that ships the category narrative
- SEO presence on the top 20 buyer-intent queries
- Branded search and direct traffic baseline

### Layer 2 — Reach (brand-building)
- Content distribution to the 95% via the channels they actually consume
- Thought leadership from the CEO, CMO, and key operators
- Sponsored content where the ICP gathers (podcasts, newsletters, communities)
- Earned media (PR, analyst coverage, customer-led)

### Layer 3 — Intent (capture)
- Paid search on commercial-intent queries
- Comparison-site presence (G2, Capterra)
- Retargeting against engaged accounts
- Sales-development outreach to in-market accounts

### Layer 4 — Account (ABM for high-ACV)
- Target account list of ~150 named accounts per AE
- Coordinated outbound + paid + content + events against the list
- 1:1 personalization for the top 25-50

### Layer 5 — Lifecycle (post-conversion)
- Onboarding sequences that drive activation
- Expansion sequences that drive upsell
- Renewal sequences that drive retention
- Win-back sequences for churn

Each layer has its own lead measure and lag measure. The CMO refuses to scale a layer that does not have both instrumented.

## 4. Sangram Vajre's ABM playbook

For B2B with ACV > $25K, Account-Based Marketing (ABM) is the dominant demand discipline. The CMO uses ABM as a complement, not a replacement, for inbound.

### 4.1 The TEAM framework

Vajre's *ABM is B2B*: **Target, Engage, Activate, Measure.**

- **Target** — define the named-accounts list with sales. Tier 1 (1:1, ~50 accounts), Tier 2 (1:few, ~200), Tier 3 (1:many, ~1000).
- **Engage** — multi-channel coordinated touches: paid (display, LinkedIn, programmatic), content (custom landing pages, gated assets), outbound (sequenced email + LinkedIn), events (executive dinners, intimate roundtables).
- **Activate** — sales-and-marketing alignment on entry triggers, hand-off SLAs, and intent thresholds.
- **Measure** — pipeline contribution by account-tier, engagement-to-pipeline conversion, deal velocity by account-status.

### 4.2 The shared scorecard

ABM only works when sales and marketing operate from one scorecard. The CMO codifies the scorecard:

- Account engagement score (composite of paid impressions, content engagement, outbound responses, event attendance)
- Account-status taxonomy (untouched / engaged / opportunity / customer / expansion)
- Hand-off SLA (engaged account → SDR outreach within 48 hours)
- Joint pipeline goals at the tier level

Without the shared scorecard, ABM becomes "marketing emails to a list" and produces nothing.

## 5. Channel architecture for common motions

### 5.1 PLG B2B (Slack-style, Notion-style)

| Layer | Primary channels |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Product-led SEO (templates, integrations, use cases) |
| Reach | Founder content, community engagement, partner integrations |
| Intent | Branded search, capterra/g2, retargeting |
| Account | Light-touch enterprise expansion playbook |
| Lifecycle | In-product activation, team-invite, paid-feature reveal |

### 5.2 SLG B2B mid-market ($25-100K ACV)

| Layer | Primary channels |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Authority content, ICP-specific landing pages, calculator/tool |
| Reach | Thought leadership, executive events, analyst influence |
| Intent | Paid search, intent data (Bombora, 6sense), comparison sites |
| Account | Tier-1 ABM with 1:1 touch, Tier-2 with 1:few |
| Lifecycle | Onboarding + customer marketing + reference program |

### 5.3 SLG B2B enterprise ($100K+ ACV)

| Layer | Primary channels |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Long-form thought leadership, customer case studies, analyst reports |
| Reach | Executive newsletter, industry events, board-level influence |
| Intent | High-touch outbound + intent data + comparison-site presence |
| Account | 1:1 ABM for full target list, deep personalization |
| Lifecycle | Customer advisory board, executive sponsor program, multi-year planning |

## 6. Lead measures by channel (the dashboard)

The CMO instruments lead measures for every channel that move within 30 days, not only lag measures that move in 90+.

| Channel | Lead measure | Lag measure |
|---|---|---|
| SEO | Pages indexed in top 10, keyword cluster coverage | Organic traffic, organic pipeline |
| Paid search | CTR, CPC, conversion rate | CAC, payback, LTV/CAC |
| LinkedIn ads | CTR, video view rate, follow growth | Pipeline, account engagement lift |
| Content | Publishing cadence, engagement per piece | Inbound MQL, branded search |
| Podcast | Downloads, episode-completion rate | Sales-influenced pipeline (self-reported) |
| Events | Registrations, attendance, post-event meetings booked | Pipeline by event-touched account |
| ABM | Account-engagement score, meetings booked | Pipeline by tier, opportunity creation |
| Outbound (SDR) | Sequences sent, reply rate, meeting rate | Sales-accepted opportunities |
| Referral program | Referrals submitted, referral activation rate | Referred-account ARR |

The CMO reviews lead measures weekly and lag measures monthly. Lag-only review is too slow to course-correct.

## 7. Common demand failure modes

- **Optimizing the form fill.** A 23% lift in conversion on a low-quality lead source produces more low-quality leads, not pipeline.
- **Last-click attribution as the only model.** Routinely under-credits brand and content; over-credits paid search and direct.
- **MQL volume as the primary metric.** MQLs are an intermediate measure. Sales-accepted opportunities and closed-won pipeline are the metric.
- **One channel as the engine.** A demand engine with one channel is a fragile demand engine. Diversification is risk management.
- **No demand creation budget.** Capture-only marketing wins share of a fixed pool. The pool does not grow.
- **No ABM for high-ACV.** Inbound at $100K+ ACV does not produce enough volume. ABM is not optional at that price point.
- **Skipping the activation layer.** Activation-stage marketing (onboarding, time-to-value) is treated as product's job. It is shared with marketing.
- **Not measuring brand contribution to demand.** Brand investment without lead measures is unfundable in the next CFO review.

## 8. Budget allocation heuristics

For a $5M annual marketing budget at a Series B B2B SaaS company, a defensible starting allocation:

| Layer | Allocation | Sub-allocation |
|---|---|---|
| People | 50% | Marketing org, agencies, contractors |
| Brand / demand creation | 20% | Content, podcast, events, thought leadership, distinctive assets |
| Demand capture | 20% | Paid search, paid social, retargeting, comparison sites |
| ABM | 7% | Intent data, ABM platform, tier-1 outbound |
| Tools and infrastructure | 3% | Analytics, MAP, ABM tools, design tools |

This is a starting point, not a prescription. The CMO adjusts based on motion, stage, and the bottleneck in the current quarter.
