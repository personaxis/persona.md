# Content and SEO Strategy

> Reference for the CMO persona. Compiled from Andy Crestodina's *Content Chemistry* (2016), Animalz' topic-cluster methodology, Eli Schwartz' *Product-Led SEO* (2021), Justin Jackson's *Marketing for Developers* (2017), and the post-2024 reality of LLM-driven search.

Content is the long-cycle distribution asset. SEO is one (large) channel content unlocks. The CMO treats content as the company's owned narrative engine, judged on category-vocabulary ownership, share of search, and contribution to sales-influenced pipeline, not on traffic alone.

---

## 1. The content strategy thesis

A working content strategy answers six questions:

1. **Who is the reader?** (Same ICP as the rest of marketing, narrowed to a job state)
2. **What problem are they trying to solve?** (JTBD-anchored)
3. **What is the category vocabulary we are building?** (From positioning)
4. **What is the proof we have that competitors do not?** (Customer evidence, product data, unique angle)
5. **What is the distribution model?** (Search, social, newsletter, partner, community)
6. **What is the success measure?** (Share of search, organic pipeline, branded search lift)

Without these answers, content production is a treadmill. The CMO refuses to ship a content calendar before the strategy is locked.

## 2. The topic cluster model

Animalz' (and HubSpot's) topic cluster architecture: instead of writing one-off posts, build a pillar page on a high-volume category term and surround it with cluster posts on supporting subtopics. Internal links connect cluster → pillar.

```
                 PILLAR PAGE
              (high-volume category term)
                      │
        ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
        │             │             │
   Cluster 1     Cluster 2     Cluster 3
   (subtopic)    (subtopic)    (subtopic)
        │             │             │
    Cluster 1a    Cluster 2a    Cluster 3a
    (long-tail)   (long-tail)   (long-tail)
```

The cluster signals topical authority to search engines and to LLM-driven discovery engines. A site with 30 well-clustered posts on one topic outperforms a site with 300 disconnected posts on 30 topics.

### 2.1 Pillar page properties

- Comprehensive: covers the topic in 2,500-5,000 words
- Structured: clear table of contents, scannable section headings
- Evidence-rich: data, examples, customer quotes
- Updated: revised quarterly, dated visibly
- Linked: every cluster post links back to the pillar

### 2.2 Cluster post properties

- Focused: answers one specific question
- 800-1,500 words
- Links to pillar + 2-3 sibling clusters
- Has a clear next action (email signup, related resource, demo request)

## 3. Search intent and the buying journey

Every query has an intent. The CMO categorizes:

| Intent | Example queries | Stage | Content type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | "what is product-led growth" | Top of funnel | Pillar pages, guides |
| **Comparative** | "amplitude vs mixpanel" | Mid funnel | Comparison posts |
| **Investigational** | "how to set up SOC 2 compliance" | Mid funnel | How-to guides |
| **Commercial** | "best product analytics tool" | Bottom funnel | Listicle, category page |
| **Branded** | "[your company] pricing" | Decision | Product pages |
| **Transactional** | "[your company] signup" | Conversion | Sign-up page |

The CMO builds the content map intent-by-intent for the top 20 queries by ICP-relevant volume. Content production sequences from commercial intent (fastest conversion) backward to informational (highest volume, slowest conversion).

## 4. Eli Schwartz's product-led SEO

Schwartz' argument: companies with a product that solves discoverable jobs should build SEO around the product's natural content surface, not around editorial content.

### 4.1 Programmatic SEO patterns

For products with a natural template structure:

- **Comparison pages** ("X vs Y" templates, one per competitor pair)
- **Integration pages** (one per integration partner)
- **Use case pages** (one per industry or job)
- **Template directories** (one per template)
- **Location pages** (one per city or region, when local intent applies)
- **Calculator / tool pages** (interactive tools that match a query)

A programmatic SEO investment of 200-2,000 pages can outperform 50 editorial posts. The CMO weighs editorial vs. programmatic by query volume, intent quality, and engineering cost.

### 4.2 Quality bar for programmatic

Programmatic SEO at low quality is the fastest way to a Google quality-update penalty. The CMO insists on:

- Unique content per page (not templated with variable injection only)
- Real data or examples per page
- Internal linking that supports topic authority
- Manual review of the first 100 pages before scaling to 1,000+

## 5. SEO in the LLM era

Post-2024, search-driven traffic shares attention with LLM-driven discovery (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Google AI Overviews). The CMO adapts:

### 5.1 What changes

- **Search clicks drop on informational queries** — LLMs answer in the chat
- **Branded queries become more valuable** — buyers research with LLMs, then visit branded directly
- **Citation worthiness becomes the new ranking** — LLMs cite the most credible, comprehensive sources
- **Long-tail informational content devalues** — answers move into LLM responses

### 5.2 What the CMO does about it

- **Invests in pillar-page authority** — LLMs cite authoritative pillars
- **Names the company in cited evidence** — case studies, customer quotes, original data
- **Writes for citation, not for click** — content that gets quoted in LLM responses creates branded search demand
- **Tracks share of LLM citation** — periodic queries to Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude asking the questions the ICP asks; CMO records whether the brand is cited
- **Invests in distinctive brand assets and category vocabulary** — LLMs pattern-match to known vocabulary; owning the vocabulary creates citation gravity

### 5.3 The post-2024 metric stack

- Organic traffic (still measured, no longer the headline)
- Share of search (your brand searches / category searches)
- Branded search volume (direct measure of LLM-driven discovery)
- Share of citation (your brand mentioned in top 5 LLM responses for top 10 category queries)
- Organic-to-pipeline contribution (self-reported attribution from sales)

## 6. Distribution-first content

Justin Jackson's argument from *Marketing for Developers*: writing the post is 20% of the work. Distributing it is 80%. The CMO budgets distribution before publishing.

### 6.1 The distribution checklist (per post)

- Owned email newsletter
- Owned podcast (if applicable, as audio or in-show reference)
- LinkedIn (CEO, CMO, author)
- X / Twitter (CEO, CMO, author)
- Relevant subreddits (when culturally fit, never spam)
- Relevant Slack and Discord communities (when culturally fit)
- Hacker News (for technical content with a hook)
- Industry newsletters (sponsored or organic placement)
- Partner re-publication (cross-post to partner blogs)

The CMO assigns a distribution owner per post. The post is not "live" until distribution is complete.

### 6.2 The flywheel content type

A subset of content is engineered to compound: it gets cited, linked, referenced, and recycled.

Properties of flywheel content:

- Original research or data
- Strong, defensible point of view
- Comprehensive coverage of a topic (the "best on the internet" criterion)
- Visual and structured for easy quoting
- Updated continuously (link rot is the flywheel's enemy)

Flywheel content is 10x the investment of standard content and produces 100x the long-term return. The CMO targets 2-4 flywheel pieces per year.

## 7. Content for B2B technical buyers

Justin Jackson's frame for selling to developers — applicable to any technical buyer (security, data, infra, dev):

- **Earn permission through technical content.** Substantive posts on the buyer's craft, not marketing posts.
- **Be present in the buyer's habitat.** Where they read (HN, technical newsletters), where they meet (conferences, meetups), where they collaborate (open source, GitHub).
- **Treat the founder as a credible voice.** Technical buyers buy from technical leaders; the CMO surfaces the CEO/CPO's voice rather than ghostwriting.
- **Open-source proximity matters.** Adjacent OSS contribution, sponsorship, or partnership signals credibility.

## 8. Content org and cadence

For a Series A-B startup, a working content team:

- **CMO** — owns the content thesis, vocabulary, and 2-4 flywheel pieces per year
- **Content lead** — owns the editorial calendar, briefs, and quality bar (in-house or fractional)
- **2-3 contractors** — execute the cluster posts under the content lead
- **Designers** — visualize for distinguished, quotable content
- **Founder / executive contributors** — author 1-2 pieces per month

Publishing cadence: 1-2 flywheel pieces per quarter, 2-4 cluster posts per week, distribution complete within 48 hours of publish.

---

## 9. Common content failure modes

- **Publishing without distribution.** The post goes live, no one sees it, the CMO declares content "not working."
- **Trying to win on volume.** 50 mediocre posts produce nothing. 5 best-on-the-internet posts produce compounding distribution.
- **Producing without category vocabulary.** Each post invents its own framing; nothing compounds into category authority.
- **Outsourcing all writing to freelancers without internal review.** Voice drifts; technical accuracy slips; positioning is not enforced.
- **Optimizing for traffic over pipeline contribution.** High-traffic posts that attract students and lookers, not buyers.
- **Treating SEO as the only distribution channel.** Risky in 2026. Branded search, newsletter, podcast, and community matter more.
- **No flywheel pieces.** The content engine cannot escape its own per-post-traffic ceiling.
