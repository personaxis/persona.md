Tier 1 launch · Continuous Audit Trail · Q3 2026

Provable continuous monitoring is the new floor.

The launch narrative for the CEO keynote, broken down stage by stage, with the surface plan that inherits it: landing page, email sequence, sales enablement, analyst program, in-product, and the dinner series.

The keynote (CEO voice, ~7 minutes)

Stage 1 · The worldview (open)
"Six months ago, the head of compliance at one of our customers told me something that stuck. She said: 'My regulator does not care that I monitored the transactions. They care that I can prove I was monitoring when something happened.' That sentence reframed how we think about compliance. The job is not monitoring. The job is provable continuous monitoring."
Why this open: a worldview before a product. Buyers retain worldviews; they forget features. The customer quote earns the right to make the argument. Land within 60 seconds.
Stage 2 · The two bad options
"For the last decade, fintechs have been forced to choose between two bad options. Option one: monitor it manually, generate the report quarterly, and hope the regulator never asks for the timeline of a specific event. Option two: license a platform built for banks, configure it for six months, and hand your engineers a vendor relationship none of them signed up for. Both options share the same gap. Neither one produces an audit trail the regulator can read in real time, without you in the room."
Why this framing: names the real competitive set (manual + legacy enterprise platform) without disrespect. Audience hears "she is describing my situation," not "she is taking shots."
Stage 3 · The shift
"What changed in 2025 and 2026 is that the regulators changed. PSD3 is coming. The FCA's continuous-monitoring guidance shipped in Q4. MAS published their version in March. Three of the largest fintech regulators on three continents independently said the same thing: 'Show us the trail.' Continuous compliance is no longer a sophistication. It is the new floor."
Why the shift: creates the trigger event for the next two quarters of inbound. Buyers in the audience are now imagining their own PSD3 conversation. Pre-ship verification required: legal review of every regulatory date and quote.
Stage 4 · Introducing the capability
"Continuous Audit Trail is the foundation of that operating model. From the moment a monitored event occurs, it is logged. The log is immutable. The log is timestamped against a verifiable clock. The log is shareable, in regulator-readable format, with a single link. When a regulator asks what you saw, when, the answer is a URL. The compliance officer does not block on engineering. The engineering team does not get a Slack message at 11pm. The regulator gets the trail in the format their internal tooling already consumes."
Why this construction: four short sentences, each a verifiable claim. "The answer is a URL" is the line that gets quoted. It is small, specific, memorable.
Stage 5 · Proof
"We worked with [Customer A], [Customer B], and [Customer C] for the last four months to test exactly this. Two of them have already used the trail in a live regulator conversation. One of them cleared an audit in eight days that, with their prior process, would have taken three weeks of preparation. The third said something I want you to hear directly..."
Risk: the third quote is the strongest, and is conditional on a regulator interaction that may not complete by launch. Mitigation: backup plan ships with two customer stories and an aggregated cohort claim ("the seven customers we worked with this quarter").
Stage 6 · The worldview, returned
"Compliance is not a quarterly cost. It is a continuous capability. We are building the platform for fintechs that intend to operate at the new floor. Continuous Audit Trail is available today. If you are at a fintech that knows the regulator is coming, talk to us. If you are at a fintech that does not yet know the regulator is coming, talk to us sooner."
Why the close: returns to the worldview the keynote opened with. Closes with a call that is direct without being needy. The last sentence is a friendly nudge to the prospect who has not yet recognized urgency.

The narrative is the company's most-quoted piece of content for the next 12 months. It teaches the buyer the vocabulary (continuous compliance, provable continuous monitoring, the new floor) so they ask the next question with our words in their mouth.

Surface 2

Dedicated launch landing page

Customer quote → the shift → what it is → how it works → competitive frame → 3 customer mini-stories → enterprise tier → CTA.

Surface 3

Customer email (existing customers)

Subject: Continuous Audit Trail is live, and what it means for your next audit.

Worldview-first. One-sentence path to enable. No demo gate.

Surface 4

Prospect 5-email sequence (ICP-fit only)

E1 the shift · E2 the cost of the old model · E3 what continuous looks like operationally · E4 customer story · E5 walkthrough invitation.

No blast. ICP-targeted only.

Surface 5

Sales enablement

Updated pitch deck · battlecard refresh · 7-min demo flow · 2 discovery trap questions · 3 customer references trained · 2 ROI calculators · sales certification by launch -1.

Surface 6

PR & analyst

3 analyst pre-briefings under NDA (Gartner, IDC, Forrester) at launch -3 weeks. One exclusive to a fintech-trade outlet at launch day. No broad-spray press release.

Surface 7

Community & field (dinner series)

CEO-led small-format dinners in London, NYC, Singapore. 8 customers + 8 prospects per dinner. No pitch. Narrative-first. Launch +14 to launch +45.

Surface 8

In-product

Customers see a single in-product callout on first sign-in: "Continuous Audit Trail is live. See your trail ›." Click leads to their own trail, not a marketing page.
