# Example output: Brand voice guidelines (v1.2)

**Context given to CMO:**
> We've never written down our brand voice. Marketing copy across the website, sales decks, emails, and product UI all sound slightly different. The team keeps asking what "our voice" actually is. Write the brand voice doc the team can actually use.

---

## CMO output

The brand voice is the artifact every marketer, designer, sales rep, and customer success operator inherits. It is the document that decides whether a sentence sounds like us. It is short on purpose — long voice guides do not get used.

This is version 1.2. Date it. The voice will evolve as the company evolves; the document evolves with it. Anything in this file overrides anything older.

---

## The one-sentence voice

> We are direct, expert, and human. We treat the reader as a peer, not a prospect.

If a piece of copy does not pass that sentence, it does not ship.

---

## Five voice dimensions

For every dimension, we are X and not Y. Knowing the contrast is more useful than knowing the value.

### 1. We are direct, not blunt

- **Direct:** "This will not work for compliance teams under 5 people."
- **Blunt:** "Solo compliance officers should not use this."

Directness names a reality without performing judgment. Bluntness pushes the reader away. The difference is whether the reader feels respected after they read it.

### 2. We are expert, not academic

- **Expert:** "PSD3 changes the audit trail expectation in three concrete ways."
- **Academic:** "The regulatory landscape under PSD3 is a complex topic that warrants careful consideration."

Expertise commits to a position and explains it. Academic hedges and never lands. Buyers want experts; they ignore academics.

### 3. We are warm, not casual

- **Warm:** "If you are six months from your first regulator engagement, we can help. Here is how."
- **Casual:** "Hey there! Regulators got you down? We've got you covered :)"

Warmth shows care for the reader's situation. Casualness performs friendliness without substance. The reader feels condescended to.

### 4. We are confident, not arrogant

- **Confident:** "We built this because the legacy platforms were not designed for fintech engineering teams."
- **Arrogant:** "Unlike the dinosaurs in this space, we actually understand what fintech engineering teams need."

Confidence states what we do and why. Arrogance disrespects competitors and erodes the reader's trust in our judgment.

### 5. We are technical, not jargon-heavy

- **Technical:** "Every monitored event lands in an immutable log, timestamped against a verifiable clock, shareable as a single URL."
- **Jargon-heavy:** "Our AI-powered, real-time, blockchain-adjacent compliance monitoring platform leverages immutable distributed ledger technology to streamline regulatory workflows."

Technical specificity gives the reader something to verify. Jargon stacking signals that the writer either does not understand the subject or is hiding the substance.

## Banned phrases

These phrases never ship. If they appear in a draft, they are cut before review.

- "Game-changer" / "10x" / "Disruptor"
- "AI-powered" (we are AI-powered. So is everyone. It does not differentiate.)
- "Solutions for [vague segment]"
- "Streamline / unlock / empower" (when used as the primary value verb)
- "Reduce risk" (says nothing the buyer can verify)
- "Get started" (as a CTA when the actual action is "book a 45-minute demo")
- "Hey there" / "Hey friend" / "Friend" (we are not friends; we are peers)
- "We are excited to announce" (the reader is not excited that we are excited)
- "In today's fast-paced world" (every world is the only world; the modifier does no work)
- "[Brand] is the leading [category]" (assert leadership; do not claim it)
- "Best-in-class" (concedes the existence of a class we did not define)
- "Worry-free" / "Hassle-free" / "Effortless" (overpromises that erode trust on first use)

## Required vocabulary

These phrases are how we own the category. We use them deliberately and consistently.

- **Continuous compliance** (the category we are building, ours to own)
- **Continuous audit trail** (the foundational capability)
- **Provable continuous monitoring** (the buyer outcome, not a feature claim)
- **Operating model** (not "workflow" — we are about how the function runs, not a single task)
- **Built for fintech engineering teams** (the buyer's question we are answering)
- **Pass the audit with confidence** (the buyer outcome, plain English)

Internal documents may abbreviate. External documents use the full forms.

## Voice by surface

The voice is the same. The register adjusts.

### Website (marketing pages)

- Direct. First-person plural ("we") sparingly.
- Headlines lead with the buyer outcome, not the feature.
- Paragraphs short, sentences mostly 12-22 words.
- One idea per section.

### Product UI

- Concise. Imperative ("Generate report" not "Click here to generate a report").
- No marketing copy in product.
- Error messages explain what happened, what to do next, and who to contact.

### Email (lifecycle and marketing)

- Personal tone but no fake personalization ("Hi {{firstName}}, I noticed you...").
- Subject lines specific and falsifiable (not "An important update").
- Sender is a real human with a real address.

### Sales enablement (decks, one-pagers, scripts)

- Same voice as web, slightly more technical.
- Reps say it in their own words; the voice constrains what they say, not how they speak.

### Customer success (in-product comms, lifecycle, support)

- Warmer. The reader is paying us; we treat them accordingly.
- Acknowledges effort the customer is making.
- Never performs apology for things that are not our fault.

### CEO and CMO public writing (blog, LinkedIn, podcast)

- Most distinct voice. Personal pronouns, real opinions, named examples.
- Carries the category narrative; reinforces the vocabulary.
- Allowed to take a position other parts of the company will not.

## The two-second test

Before publishing any external copy, the author reads it aloud once and asks two questions:

1. Would I send this to a compliance officer I respect and not feel embarrassed?
2. Does any sentence in here sound like marketing was trying too hard?

If the answer to (1) is no, rewrite. If the answer to (2) is yes, cut that sentence.

---

## Voice review cadence

The CMO reviews the brand voice guide:

- **Quarterly** — light review, banned phrases updated, do/don't pairs refreshed
- **Annually** — full review, dimensions revisited, sample copy from the year audited for drift
- **On positioning change** — full rewrite

Every new marketer reads the guide on day one. Every new sales hire reads it during enablement. Designers read the relevant sections before any external surface ships.

The guide is the contract. It is updated when reality changes; it is enforced when copy drifts.

---

## What this guide does not cover

- Visual identity (logo, type, color, layout). Owned by design.
- Editorial style (Oxford comma, capitalization, hyphenation). Owned by the editorial standard guide.
- Legal and regulatory copy requirements. Owned by legal review.
- Localization. Voice translates; idiom does not. Localized copy follows local writers who understand both this voice and the target language.
