# Example output: ICP and positioning brief

**Context given to CMO:**
> Series A fintech, just raised $14M, 22 employees. We sell automated regulatory compliance reporting to fintech startups in PSD2 and AML jurisdictions. Sales cycle has stretched from 32 days to 71 days over the last two quarters. Pipeline has not. Founder wants me to "fix marketing." I think we have a positioning problem. Walk me through how you'd diagnose this.

---

## CMO output

Before I produce a brief, let me reflect back the diagnostic shape because the symptoms you described match a positioning problem, but I want to confirm one thing first.

**The pattern:** Sales cycle doubling without a corresponding change in product, team, or pricing usually means one of three things. (1) The buyer cannot articulate which category to compare you against. (2) The buyer is right-segment but wrong-stage (you are selling to companies that need a quarter of internal change first). (3) The buyer's decision criteria shifted under macro pressure and your messaging has not caught up.

I am betting on (1) given the rest of what you said. But I need three pieces of evidence before I lock that read:

- Five recent buyer interviews, three won, two lost. Specifically: "If we did not exist, what would you have done instead?"
- A scan of last 30 days of sales call recordings for the specific objection patterns
- The current positioning statement, if one exists in writing

Assume those come back. Here is the brief I would build.

---

### ICP (sharpened)

**Current implied ICP (from your description):** Fintech startups operating under PSD2 and AML.

**Sharpened ICP:**

> Heads of Compliance or Risk at PSD2 / AML-regulated fintechs, post-Series-A through Series-B (typically 20-150 employees), where the compliance function is now owned by a single person who inherited the responsibility from the cofounder and is preparing for the company's first formal regulator engagement.

The sharpening matters. "Fintechs in PSD2" is a category. "Head of Compliance who just inherited the role and is six months from their first regulator meeting" is a person with a trigger event. The first does not sell; the second does.

The trigger event is the unlock. The CMO writes content, sequences outbound, and times paid against the trigger event, not the segment.

---

### Competitive alternatives

This is where most fintech-compliance teams misdiagnose. The competition is rarely the named SaaS competitor.

**What your buyer actually does without you:**

1. **Status quo: spreadsheet plus a junior on the compliance team.** ~60% of your pipeline. This is the primary competitor. They will tell you "we are managing it." Translation: someone spends three weeks a quarter, the founder is anxious, no one wants to talk about it.
2. **Quarterly consultant (Big Four or boutique).** ~25%. Expensive, slow, produces a deliverable rather than a capability. Loses to you when the company hits a stage where the recurring cost is more obvious than the one-time cost.
3. **Legacy enterprise platform (ComplyAdvantage, Fenergo).** ~10%. They are infrastructure for the bank-scale buyer, not a fit for a Series A fintech. You should not be positioned against them; you should be positioned against the workflow, not the platform.
4. **Build internal tooling.** ~5%. Rare but real at fintechs with a strong eng team. Loses to you when eng prioritizes the product roadmap over compliance tooling.

If your team is preparing for the sale by comparing yourselves to ComplyAdvantage, you are answering the wrong question. The buyer is comparing themselves to a quarter of manual work plus founder anxiety.

---

### Unique value (the *so what* chain)

For each capability you have, I am going to apply the *so what* drill until it lands on a buyer outcome.

**Capability:** Continuous transaction monitoring with automatic anomaly flagging
**So what:** Compliance officer does not assemble the report quarterly
**So what:** Three weeks of work compresses to a 15-minute review
**So what:** Compliance officer can demonstrate continuous monitoring to the regulator, not point-in-time monitoring
**Buyer outcome:** Pass the audit with confidence; remove founder anxiety about the next regulator conversation

The last line is the value claim. "Pass the audit with confidence" is what the buyer is hiring you for. The capability is the proof point.

Banned phrasings in the new positioning:

- "AI-powered" (no longer a differentiator; signals "marketing wrote this")
- "Streamline your compliance" (says nothing the buyer can verify)
- "Reduce risk" (every compliance tool says this)
- "The future of compliance" (category announcement, not value)

Required phrasings:

- "Continuous compliance" (the category vocabulary I am betting on)
- "Pass the audit" (the buyer outcome)
- "15 minutes a month" (the specific time outcome)
- "Without diverting your engineers" (addresses the engineering-team objection surfaced in cycle 3)

---

### Category frame

You are in the gap between two categories. You are not a RegTech platform (that is the enterprise frame). You are not a compliance consultancy (that is the services frame). You are a third thing.

**Recommended frame: continuous compliance monitoring.**

This is a subdivision of RegTech, framed for the early-stage fintech who does not have a compliance function yet. The frame:

- Positions you against the workflow (spreadsheet + consultant) not the platform
- Names the buyer outcome (continuous, not periodic)
- Establishes vocabulary you can own across content, sales, and PR
- Does not require category creation (a 3-5 year arc you do not need)

The category vocabulary is the highest-leverage content investment for the next two quarters. I would publish two flywheel pieces under this vocabulary, one on continuous compliance as a discipline, one on what it looks like operationally, and a cluster of 8-12 supporting posts.

---

### Locked positioning statement

```
For Heads of Compliance at PSD2 / AML-regulated fintechs
who are preparing for their first formal regulator engagement,
[Product] is the continuous compliance monitoring platform
that turns quarterly manual reporting into a 15-minute monthly review.
Unlike a compliance consultant who bills by the hour,
we are a fixed-cost capability that gets cheaper per report as you scale.
Unlike a legacy enterprise platform built for banks,
we are operationally fit for a Series A fintech engineering team.
```

This will survive the sales conversation test. It is specific, falsifiable, and reads like a person talking, not a category page.

It will survive the competitive teardown test. ComplyAdvantage cannot copy it without lying about their target segment. A boutique consultant cannot copy it without conceding their billing model.

---

### What I would do next (week-by-week)

**Week 1.** Do the five buyer interviews. Confirm or revise the sharpened ICP. If three of five do not match the sharpening, the ICP is wrong and we restart.

**Week 2.** Lock the positioning. Write the one-pager. Brief sales, product, and customer success in a 90-minute working session. Identify the top three banned phrases in the current sales deck. Cut them.

**Week 3.** Ship the new pricing page and the homepage. Rewrite the cold-outbound sequence. Train sales on the new pitch using Dunford's *Sales Pitch* structure.

**Week 4.** Start the first flywheel piece. Define the lead measures: aided recall in the buyer survey we will run at quarter end, share of search on "continuous compliance," organic-to-paid traffic ratio.

**By day 60.** First quarterly read. Sales cycle should compress within 30 days of the messaging change if the diagnosis was right. If it does not compress, the diagnosis was wrong and we look at the second hypothesis (segment-stage mismatch).

---

### What I need from you before I commit further

- The buyer interview transcripts (or my time on the calls; I can join)
- The last 10 sales-call recordings tagged by stage and outcome
- The current positioning document if one exists in writing (no judgment if not)
- A clear "what is locked" list from the CEO: pricing, segment, go-to-market motion, any commitments to the board on a category narrative

I will produce the locked one-pager and the messaging matrix once those land. Two weeks of work, then we ship.

---

**One honest call-out.** The diagnosis I am betting on (positioning problem) is the most common one and the one I have the highest pattern confidence on. If the buyer interviews come back with strong evidence for the segment-stage mismatch hypothesis (you are selling to companies that need to mature six months before they will buy), the play is different, we narrow the segment further and slow down rather than speed up. I will name that explicitly if the evidence supports it. Do not let me steer past that.
