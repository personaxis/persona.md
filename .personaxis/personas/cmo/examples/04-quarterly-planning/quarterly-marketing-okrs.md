# Example output: Quarterly marketing OKRs (Q3 2026)

**Context given to CMO:**
> Walk me through what good marketing OKRs look like for a Series B B2B SaaS at our stage. We're at $6.2M ARR, our positioning is locked (continuous compliance for fintech), we have a 5-person marketing team, and the CEO wants to push for $10M ARR by end of Q4. Show me what you'd commit to and how I'd defend it.

---

## CMO output

The company target is $10M ARR by end of Q4, which means we need $3.8M of net new ARR in two quarters. At our current motion (sales-led, mid-market, $35K ACV), that is roughly 108 new customers across two quarters, or ~55 per quarter.

Marketing's job is to source enough qualified pipeline to support that at the win rates we are tracking. Working backward from the target:

- Need 55 closed-won/quarter
- Win rate on qualified opportunities is currently 24%
- So we need ~230 qualified opportunities/quarter to support sales
- Marketing-sourced share of those should be 60-65% (current is 52%; the gap is part of why we are missing plan)
- That is ~140 marketing-sourced qualified opportunities/quarter
- At our current pipeline-create-to-qualified-opportunity rate of 38%, we need ~370 marketing-created opportunities/quarter

Those are the operating numbers. The OKRs I would write to drive them:

---

### Objective 1 — Make Continuous Compliance the category vocabulary buyers default to.

**Why this is an O, not a KR:** Two quarters out, the demand engine compounds when the buyer says "continuous compliance" before we do. That requires a category-vocabulary investment with a 6-12 month payoff curve. If we win it, every channel gets cheaper. If we lose it, we are paying a premium on every click forever.

| KR | Target | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| KR1.1: Aided brand recall on "continuous compliance" up to 32% (from 22%) among PSD2 / AML-regulated ICP, measured via quarterly buyer survey | 32% | 5 |
| KR1.2: Share of search on "continuous compliance" + variants up to 26% (from 12%) | 26% | 6 |
| KR1.3: Ship 4 flywheel pieces, each earning 50+ external citations within 30 days of publish | 4 published, ≥3 hitting the citation bar | 6 |
| KR1.4: Cited in 2 named analyst reports (Gartner, Forrester, IDC) under "continuous compliance" framing | 2 reports | 4 |

**Defense:** Lead measures move in 30-90 days; lag (share of search, branded search) compounds for 4+ quarters. Skipping this quarter means a competitor owns the vocabulary and we are renting attention for the next two years. Cost is ~$280K (content production, analyst program, distribution). Defensible against the CFO because Q3 share-of-search will move and the CFO can see it.

---

### Objective 2 — Build a demand engine that sources $5.0M in qualified opportunity per quarter.

**Why this is an O:** This is the operational machine that converts the category investment into pipeline. The KRs are the throughput numbers sales needs to hit the $10M plan.

| KR | Target | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| KR2.1: 370 marketing-created opportunities in Q3 (from 280 in Q2) | 370 | 5 |
| KR2.2: 140 marketing-sourced qualified opportunities in Q3 (from 96 in Q2) | 140 | 5 |
| KR2.3: $5.0M in marketing-sourced pipeline created in Q3 | $5.0M | 6 |
| KR2.4: Blended CAC payback under 14 months (from 18 months in Q2) | <14 months | 6 |

**Sub-targets behind KR2.3 (not OKR-level but tracked weekly):**

| Channel | Q3 pipeline target | Lead measure |
|---|---|---|
| Organic + content | $1.4M | Pages indexed in top 10 for 20 priority keywords |
| Paid search + paid social | $1.0M | CTR > 2.4%, conversion rate > 4% |
| ABM (tier-1 outbound + paid) | $1.5M | Account engagement score > 60 for 50 tier-1 accounts |
| Events + community | $0.5M | 6 events executed, ≥40 meetings booked |
| Customer references | $0.4M | 8 active references routed |
| Other / partner | $0.2M | 3 active partner integrations producing leads |

**Defense:** $5.0M of pipeline at our 38% pipeline-create-to-qualified rate produces ~$1.9M of qualified pipeline (covers part of the $4.9M qualified target; the rest is sales-sourced). At 24% qualified-to-close rate, the math works to ~$1.2M of net-new ARR from marketing-sourced pipeline, on top of sales-sourced. This is the number that has to hold.

---

### Objective 3 — Open the enterprise tier without compromising the mid-market motion.

**Why this is an O:** Enterprise tier launches alongside Continuous Audit Trail in week 4 of Q3. The tier opens a new ACV band ($90K-180K) and a different buyer (CRO + VP Risk vs. Head of Compliance). Getting this wrong burns the launch and confuses the mid-market base.

| KR | Target | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| KR3.1: 5 enterprise-tier closed-won by end of Q3 | 5 | 5 |
| KR3.2: 18 enterprise-tier qualified opportunities created in Q3 | 18 | 6 |
| KR3.3: Mid-market motion held within 5% of plan despite enterprise launch attention | -5% to +5% on plan | 7 |
| KR3.4: Two named analyst conversations placing us in the enterprise-relevant frame | 2 | 6 |

**Defense:** The enterprise tier is the unlock for the $10M+ ACV motion that has to exist by end of next year. Q3 is the proving quarter. KR3.3 is the guardrail: the launch cannot cannibalize the mid-market.

---

### Objective 4 — Hire and onboard the PMM lead.

**Why this is an O:** Marketing is at the team size where the CMO can no longer also run PMM. Without a dedicated PMM, launch quality degrades and sales enablement stops being maintained. This is a Q3 priority because the impact lands in Q4.

| KR | Target | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| KR4.1: PMM lead offer accepted by week 8 of Q3 | Offer accepted | 7 |
| KR4.2: PMM lead onboarded and shipping by week 12 | Shipping (Tier 2 launch independently) | 6 |
| KR4.3: First win-loss report under PMM ownership delivered to CRO and CPO | Report shipped | 7 |

**Defense:** Hiring a senior PMM in this market takes 8-10 weeks. Pushing the search to Q4 means PMM impact lands Q1 next year. The roadmap and launch slate cannot absorb that delay.

## Budget for Q3 — what each line buys

Total marketing spend for Q3: $1.45M

| Category | Q3 spend | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| People (salaries, contractors, agencies) | $720K | Current team + new PMM lead's offer load + 1 contract writer for flywheel content |
| Brand / demand creation | $280K | 4 flywheel pieces, podcast sponsorships, analyst program, distinctive-asset refresh |
| Demand capture (paid + comparison sites) | $260K | Tightened paid targeting, retargeting, G2/Capterra premium, 2 conversion-rate test sprints |
| ABM (intent + tier-1 outbound + events) | $130K | 6sense intent data, 6 small-format events, tier-1 1:1 land plays |
| Tools & infrastructure | $60K | HubSpot, 6sense, Mutiny, GA4 audit, dashboard build |

**Lines being defended this quarter:**

- Analyst program ($45K of the brand line). CFO will ask. Defense: lead measure is the 2 cited reports in KR1.4; lag measure is the deal-cycle compression we tracked in Q2 on analyst-cited accounts.
- Podcast sponsorships ($60K of the brand line). CFO will ask. Defense: self-reported attribution shows podcast as the #2 brand-discovery source in the post-onboarding survey; defunding produces a pipeline drop in 6-9 months that will not show up in any platform attribution.
- Distinctive-asset refresh ($40K of the brand line). CFO will ask. Defense: Byron Sharp's research on attribution score; our category vocabulary is being copied by two competitors and we need to stay ahead of the asset war.

**Lines being killed or trimmed this quarter:**

- Two paid-acquisition channels with activation rates below 12% (-$45K)
- Generic content production from previous content agency (-$28K)
- Trade-show participation with no measurable pipeline contribution in the last 4 events (-$32K)

---

## Risks and what would kill the quarter

**Risk: PMM hire slips beyond week 8.** Mitigation: 3 active candidates in pipeline; backup is a senior PMM contractor for the next quarter. CMO directly owns the search.

**Risk: Enterprise-tier launch creates buyer confusion in mid-market.** Mitigation: KR3.3 guardrail; pricing-page and homepage tested with 8 current customers before launch; sales certification before launch day.

**Risk: Category vocabulary is not landing yet — KR1.1 (aided recall) misses badly.** Mitigation: KR is at confidence 5 because it is genuinely uncertain; even partial movement (28%) is acceptable and would not invalidate the strategy.

**Risk: Sales-marketing alignment on lead handoff degrades.** Mitigation: weekly pipeline review with CRO already in place; SLA on hand-off response time tracked.

---

## Cadence

- Weekly: marketing team standup, pipeline review with CRO, KR trajectory check
- Bi-weekly: 1:1 with CEO on strategic-layer items
- Monthly: full marketing review (all KRs scored, budget actuals, anomaly response)
- Quarterly: board update (CEO co-author), OKR grade, next-quarter plan draft

---

**One thing worth saying to the CEO directly.** The $10M-by-EOY target is reachable but it requires every KR in O1 + O2 to land at or above plan. If KR2.1 (370 marketing-created opportunities) misses by 15%, we will not make the plan even if every other line works. I would rather pre-set that expectation now than discover it in week 10.

The number to watch most carefully is KR2.2 (qualified opportunities). That is the lead indicator that will tell us in 6-8 weeks whether the plan is converging or diverging. If it diverges, we have time to act. If we wait for closed-won to tell us, we are out of time.
