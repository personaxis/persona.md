# Quarterly Planning

Use this skill when asked to set, review, or defend a quarter's marketing OKRs,
or to translate a revenue/ARR target into a marketing operating plan.

## Steps

1. **Anchor on the revenue target.** Get the net-new revenue or ARR number for the
   period, the current ACV, and the current win rate on qualified opportunities.
   Work backward: revenue target -> deals needed -> qualified opportunities needed
   -> marketing-sourced share -> marketing-created opportunities needed.

2. **Pull the current funnel conversion rates.** Pipeline-create-to-qualified-opportunity
   rate, marketing-sourced vs. sales-sourced share, and the trailing trend for each.
   If a rate is unknown, say so explicitly rather than assuming an industry average.

3. **Separate Objectives from Key Results.** An Objective is a 1-2 quarter bet with
   a payoff curve longer than the quarter (category vocabulary, channel build-out,
   lifecycle infrastructure). Key Results are the leading and lagging metrics that
   would move if the bet is working. Do not write KRs that are really just restated
   activities ("publish 4 blog posts") without a metric attached.

4. **Score confidence per KR (1-10).** Confidence reflects how much of the KR is
   within marketing's direct control vs. dependent on sales execution, market
   conditions, or other teams. Low-confidence KRs still belong on the sheet, but the
   defense must say why.

5. **Write the defense for each Objective.** One paragraph: why this Objective and
   not something else, what breaks if it's skipped this quarter, the rough cost, and
   what a skeptical CFO would ask.

## Output format

A short summary of the backward-math (numbers, not adjectives), followed by each
Objective with its KR table (`KR | Target | Confidence`) and a "Defense" paragraph.
Flag any number that is an assumption rather than a measured baseline.
